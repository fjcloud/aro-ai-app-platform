== Connect an app action to the graphs

. Keep the dashboard open in one tab and *Card Scout* in another.
. Choose an analyzed Pokémon card whose price you have not checked yet and click
  *Check price*. Note the time.
. After the next scrapes, find the increase for `deepseekv4pro-card-scout` in the
  token and request panels. The coding subscription should not increase because
  of this action.
. Compare with the T4 panels: price research uses DeepSeek and the MCP tool,
  while D3 was used earlier for image classification.

Completed analysis and successful price research are cached. Reopening saved
results does not generate another inference request. A short request may also
fall between GPU samples; low utilization alone does not mean it never ran.

== Understand the limits and cost

In *OpenShift AI → Settings → MaaS governance → Subscriptions*, find the coding and runtime
subscriptions. Click the count in each row’s *Models* column to see its token
limit and time window. These are administrator views; use the `workshop-admin` session. Their allowances are ten million and one million tokens per
24 hours respectively. They govern consumption, not spending caps; the shared
coding key reports workshop-level usage rather than individual participants.

The dashboard is not an Azure invoice. Its token total does not separate input,
cached input and output rates. Foundry token pricing comes from your Azure
agreement; the T4 VM, CPU workers and disks remain provisioned while idle.
